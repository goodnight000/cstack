# Editorial collage

Newspaper clippings, cut and torn paper, halftone photos, maps and typed labels assembled on a
paper board, the look of Vox explainers and Johnny Harris documentaries. It reads as evidence
being laid out by hand, so it suits investigations, history, money and politics. Palette,
paper stock and density are chosen per brief.

## Its bar (in addition to the finish bar)
- **Real artifacts.** Headlines, documents, maps and photos of the actual subject, laid out
  like evidence on a desk. Draw clippings in code (headline type, columns of body text,
  halftone dot fields) or treat sourced images in code; sourced images must be the user's own,
  public domain or licensed, and are listed at delivery.
- **Cutouts.** Every element has a cut edge: a white or off-white border, a slightly irregular
  or torn outline, a soft drop shadow, so it sits on the board as paper.
- **Annotation.** Hand marks do the pointing: a marker circle, an underline, an arrow that
  draws on. One hot accent (often red) means "look here" and nothing else.
- **Motion as placement.** Paper never fades in. Pieces slide or drop into place as if a hand
  set them, overshoot slightly and settle; pins drop and wobble once; labels type on. Animate on
  twos (12 fps) for the stutter, with a slow camera drift of about 2% and small push-ins.
- **Depth.** Stacked paper layers, parallax between them, and shadows that grow as a piece
  lifts.
- **Pace.** A new piece or move every 1–3 s on the narration, each one answering what was just
  said.

## Typical failure
A slideshow: one static collage per line with a crossfade. The board has to be built, marked
up and moved through, piece by piece.
